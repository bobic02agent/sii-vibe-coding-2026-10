# Demonstration Complaint Procedure

Document ID: DEMO-COMPLAINTS. Version: 1.0. Effective for synthetic demonstrations from 2026-10-18.

These are fictional company rules for the MVP. They do not describe statutory rights, warranties, or an actual company's obligations. Do not use them for real customer decisions. Acceptance below means admission to assessment, not approval of compensation.

## Rules

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| C-01 | Handle one electronics item per complaint with category, model, purchase date, a nonblank reason, and one usable equipment image. Missing intake facts require additional information. |
| C-02 | Distinguish employee-reported symptoms from visible image findings. Absence of visible damage does not invalidate a reported functional defect. |
| C-03 | If a fault or defect is reported and no specialist or explicit refusal rule applies, recommend accepting the item for complaint assessment. State that examination determines the cause and available remedy. |
| C-04 | Possible impact, liquid exposure, wear, or misuse inferred from a photograph remains a hypothesis. It cannot alone justify refusal. If determining cause is necessary to resolve the case, recommend specialist assessment. |
| C-05 | Recommend refusal under this demonstration procedure only if the employee explicitly confirms both that accidental damage is the sole reason for the case and that the request is exclusively for free repair of that accident. Do not infer these facts. This rule does not assess any statutory or warranty claim. |
| C-06 | If the description does not identify any fault or defect, request the symptom and circumstances. If the employee explicitly confirms there is no fault and the request is solely a change-of-mind return, recommend refusal of complaint intake and starting a new return case. Do not silently switch procedures. |
| C-07 | Material contradictions, suspected safety hazards, unresolved cause-dependent eligibility, or questions outside these rules require specialist assessment. Safety concerns take priority over refusal or admission. |
| C-08 | Do not infer an eligibility period from purchase age. This demonstration procedure has no age-based refusal rule. Questions about warranty or legal deadlines require specialist review outside the demo rules. |
| C-09 | No recommendation authorizes a repair, replacement, refund, shipping, or actual complaint registration. The employee is responsible for the final decision and operational follow-through. |

## Application order

1. Check safety concerns and material contradictions under C-07.
2. Check whether explicit facts establish C-05 or the confirmed return-only situation in C-06.
3. Request missing facts required by C-01 or C-06.
4. Escalate unresolved cause-dependent questions under C-04/C-07.
5. Otherwise apply C-03 to the reported defect.

## Synthetic examples

- A laptop reportedly shuts down; the photograph shows no visible damage. Recommend admission to assessment under C-02/C-03, without diagnosing the fault.
- A cracked phone is photographed, but the cause and relation to the reported fault are disputed. Recommend specialist assessment under C-04/C-07; do not claim the employee or customer caused the crack.
- The employee confirms a device was accidentally dropped, the accidental break is the sole issue, and only free repair of that accident is requested. Recommend refusal under C-05, explicitly limited to these fictional rules.
- The employee says the device is fully functional and the only reason is a change of mind. Ask confirmation if necessary, then apply C-06 and suggest a separate return case.
